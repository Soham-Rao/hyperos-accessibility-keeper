## Your choices and powerful access

Keeper actively restores or rebinds accessibility services you select; it does more than monitor. Only select third-party apps you trust and have intentionally authorized. Restoring accessibility can restore those apps' ability to inspect screen content, perform gestures or interact with other apps, according to their capabilities. Their developers and privacy policies are separate from this project.

WRITE_SECURE_SETTINGS and DUMP permit more than Keeper's intended recovery and diagnostic uses. Read the permission guide before granting them. You can revoke permissions, stop protection or uninstall Keeper. Do not use the app to bypass another person's choices or device-administration rules.

## Controls and operational limits

Turning a protection switch off stops subsequent recovery for that service and leaves its Android setting unchanged. A rebind already in progress may finish restoring the service over the next few seconds. Turn protection off, allow any current recovery to finish, then deliberately disable the service in Android Settings.

Pause suspends subsequent recovery for 30 minutes and then expires. Force-stopping Keeper stops its work until Android allows it to run again, normally after you reopen it. Revoke its write grant to prevent further secure-settings writes. Uninstalling Keeper does not intentionally disable other apps' existing accessibility access; manage those settings separately.

Recovery, continuous operation, compatibility and safety are not guaranteed. Firmware restrictions, app bugs, permission changes, diagnostic formats and OS updates may prevent recovery. Enabled settings or a bound service do not prove every feature inside another app works. Some apps need opening manually. Do not rely on Keeper for emergency, safety-critical or uninterrupted accessibility availability.
